Hipertensión Intraabdominal (HIA) y Síndrome Compartimental Abdominal (SCA): Definiciones y Guías de Práctica Clínica Actualizadas

Este documento resume las definiciones de consenso y las guías de práctica clínica actualizadas de la World Society of the Abdominal Compartment Syndrome (WSACS), publicadas en 2013 [1, 2]. El objetivo fue revisar la literatura reciente y actualizar las declaraciones de 2006 y 2007, utilizando el sistema GRADE (Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development, and Evaluation) para calificar la calidad de la evidencia y la fuerza de las recomendaciones [3-5].

Métodos de Actualización

Se llevaron a cabo revisiones sistemáticas para identificar estudios relevantes sobre HIA y SCA [3]. Las definiciones de consenso de 2006 se evaluaron mediante un método Delphi modificado, reteniendo aquellas con más del 80% de aceptación y revisando las que tenían entre 50-80% [6]. Para las guías de manejo, se formularon preguntas clínicas estructuradas (formato PICO) y se utilizó el sistema GRADE para desarrollar declaraciones, calificando la evidencia de muy baja (D) a alta (A) [5, 7].

Resultados Clave: Definiciones, Factores de Riesgo y Clasificaciones

Definiciones de Consenso 2013 (Adultos)

La WSACS retuvo varias definiciones clave de 2006 e introdujo nuevos conceptos para mejorar la comprensión y el manejo de estas condiciones [8].

Definiciones Pediátricas Adaptadas

Un subcomité pediátrico evaluó las definiciones de adultos, aceptando la mayoría y proponiendo cuatro definiciones específicas para niños [12].

Tabla 4: Definiciones Pediátricas de Consenso Adaptadas 2013 [12]
Definición Específica Pediátrica

Declaraciones de Manejo y Recomendaciones Clínicas

A continuación se presentan las recomendaciones y sugerencias de manejo clínico de la WSACS, clasificadas y coloreadas según la fuerza de la recomendación [13-15].

Recomendaciones (GRADE 1)

Recomendación 1: We recommend measuring IAP when any known risk factor for IAH/ACS is present in a critically ill or injured patient [GRADE 1C] [14, 16].
Recomendación 2: Studies should adopt the trans-bladder technique as the standard IAP measurement technique [not GRADED] [14, 16].
Recomendación 3: We recommend use of protocolized monitoring and management of IAP versus not [GRADE 1C] [14, 16].
Recomendación 4: We recommend efforts and/or protocols to avoid sustained IAH as compared to inattention to IAP among critically ill or injured patients [GRADE 1C] [14, 17].
Recomendación 5: We recommend decompressive laparotomy in cases of overt ACS compared to strategies that do not use decompressive laparotomy in critically ill adults with ACS [GRADE 1D] [14, 18].
Recomendación 6: We recommend that among ICU patients with open abdominal wounds, conscious and/or protocolized efforts be made to obtain an early or at least same-hospital-stay abdominal fascial closure [GRADE 1D] [14, 19].
Recomendación 7: We recommend that among critically ill/injured patients with open abdominal wounds, strategies utilizing negative pressure wound therapy should be used versus not [GRADE 1C] [14, 20].

Sugerencias (GRADE 2)

Sugerencia 1: We suggest that clinicians ensure that critically ill or injured patients receive optimal pain and anxiety relief [GRADE 2D] [14, 21].
Sugerencia 2: We suggest brief trials of neuromuscular blockade as a temporizing measure in the treatment of IAH/ACS [GRADE 2D] [14, 22].
Sugerencia 3: We suggest that the potential contribution of body position to elevated IAP be considered among patients with, or at risk of, IAH or ACS [GRADE 2D] [14, 22].
Sugerencia 4: We suggest liberal use of enteral decompression with nasogastric or rectal tubes when the stomach or colon are dilated in the presence of IAH/ACS [GRADE 1D] [14, 23].
Sugerencia 5: We suggest that neostigmine be used for the treatment of established colonic ileus not responding to other simple measures and associated with IAH [GRADE 2D] [14, 24].
Sugerencia 6: We suggest using a protocol to try and avoid a positive cumulative fluid balance in the critically ill or injured patient with, or at risk of, IAH/ACS after the acute resuscitation has been completed and the inciting issues have been addressed [GRADE 2C] [14, 25].
Sugerencia 7: We suggest use of an enhanced ratio of plasma/packed red blood cells for resuscitation of massive hemorrhage versus low or no attention to plasma/packed red blood cell ratios [GRADE 2D] [14, 26].
Sugerencia 8: We suggest use of PCD to remove fluid (in the setting of obvious intraperitoneal fluid) in those with IAH/ACS when this is technically possible compared to doing nothing [GRADE 2C]. We also suggest using PCD to remove fluid (in the setting of obvious intraperitoneal fluid) in those with IAH/ACS when this is technically possible compared to immediate decompressive laparotomy as this may alleviate the need for decompressive laparotomy [GRADE 2D] [14, 27].
Sugerencia 9: We suggest that patients undergoing laparotomy for trauma suffering from physiologic exhaustion be treated with the prophylactic use of the open abdomen versus intraoperative abdominal fascial closure and expectant IAP management [GRADE 2D] [14, 28].

Sugerencias en Contra (GRADE 2)

Sugerencia 10: We suggest not to routinely utilize the open abdomen for patients with severe intraperitoneal contamination undergoing emergency laparotomy for intra-abdominal sepsis unless IAH is a specific concern [GRADE 2B] [14, 29].
Sugerencia 11: We suggest that bioprosthetic meshes should not be routinely used in the early closure of the open abdomen compared to alternative strategies [GRADE 2D] [14, 30].

Sin Recomendación

1. Presión de Perfusión Abdominal (PPA): We could make no recommendation regarding use of abdominal perfusion pressure in the resuscitation or management of the critically ill or injured [15, 31].
2. Diuréticos: We could make no recommendation regarding use of diuretics to mobilize fluids in hemodynamically stable patients with IAH after the acute resuscitation has been completed... [15, 32].
3. Terapias de reemplazo renal: We could make no recommendation regarding the use of renal replacement therapies to mobilize fluid... [15, 33].
4. Albúmina: We could make no recommendation regarding the administration of albumin versus not, to mobilize fluid... [15, 34].
5. Abdomen abierto profiláctico (no trauma): We could make no recommendation regarding the prophylactic use of the open abdomen in non-trauma acute care surgery patients with physiologic exhaustion... [15, 35].
6. Separación de componentes: We could make no recommendation regarding use of an acute component separation technique versus not to facilitate earlier abdominal fascial closure [15, 36].

Discusión y Conclusión

Los autores concluyen que, aunque la HIA y el SCA son comunes y se asocian con malos resultados, la calidad general de la evidencia disponible para guiar las recomendaciones fue generalmente baja [38, 39]. Se necesitan urgentemente ensayos de intervención bien diseñados [38]. El sistema GRADE refleja tanto la calidad de la evidencia como la experiencia colectiva del panel de expertos [39]. Estas guías deben usarse como una orientación para el cuidado clínico y no como medidas de rendimiento punitivas [39]. También se destaca la necesidad de más investigación en poblaciones específicas como pacientes pediátricos, obesos y embarazadas [40].
